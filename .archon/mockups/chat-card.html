Chat card — the mark and the gap

Two changes in one picture. Idle becomes a dot, so the three states are one vocabulary instead of two glyphs and a bubble — and it matches what the chat's own status pill and the project chip already draw. The gap closes: today the dot sits in a 15px slot with an 8px gap after it, so an 8px dot ends up ~11.5px from the word it belongs to and reads as unrelated to it. The working dot still throbs and sends a ring out, so watch that the ring does not crowd the text at the tighter gaps.

A · today

15px slot, 8px gap. Idle is a chat bubble. The mark floats away from its own label.

B · all dots, slot = dot · recommended

Slot collapses to the dot's own 8px, gap 7px. Dot edge to text: 7px, down from 11.5. Ring clears the text by 2.6px.

C · tighter

7px dot, 6px gap. Reads as one unit — but the working ring now grazes the title, and a 7px amber dot is a small thing to be the loudest signal in the rail.

D · B, in the drag gutter

Same as B, but the 17px reserved for the drag handle is given back — the dot moves left, every card gains 8px of title. The grip would then overlay the dot on hover.

What is not up for grabs here. The mark stays distinguishable without colour — working throbs and radiates, awaiting throbs alone, idle is still. Three states told apart by hue would fail for anyone who cannot separate blue from amber, and fail again in a screenshot.
Removed in the same pass: the 72% context reading on each card. The gauge in the chat's own status pill stays — it is the one that names the model and the denominator, which is what makes a percentage mean anything.
